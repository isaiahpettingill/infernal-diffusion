An armored goblin with four arms and a flaming spear riding a giant horned wolf. The goblin has a dark green face, two swept horns, pointed ears, yellow eyes, a narrow jaw and a broken lower tusk. The upper right hand carries the spear. A round iron shield with a pale rim hangs from the upper left arm. The two lower hands hold the reins. Give the rider a short red cloak, a dark leather harness, a belt with copper buckles, steel shoulder plates and boots with pale stitching. Keep the face visible between the horns and the collar. Make the back plate broad enough to read from behind. Separate the hands from the shield and leave a visible gap between the spear shaft and the cheek.

The mount has a charcoal coat, silver fur around the jaw, amber eyes, four strong legs, a thick neck and a long bushy tail. Put short ivory horns above its ears. The front paws are wide, with dark claws and a light patch of fur over the left ankle. Each joint should read clearly when the animal crouches. The tail lifts during the run, with the tip trailing behind the hips. Use a low saddle with a red blanket, a leather chest strap and two short stirrups. The blanket ends before the rear legs. Give the head a long muzzle and show the lower teeth only when the mouth opens. The muzzle should be easy to see against the chest in the front view.

The goblin stands tall in the idle pose. The wolf lowers its head before charging. On the first stride the front legs reach forward while the rider draws the spear back over one shoulder. At contact the rider drives the flaming point forward while the lower hands stay by the saddle. On recovery the shield draws close to the chest, the spear tip rises, and the mount returns to a low stance. In a side view, leave space between the two forelegs and show the rider's near elbow. In a rear view, let the edge of the shield and the two horns appear on either side of the helmet. The cloak follows the last turn of the torso. Keep the small straps, buckles and patches darker than the edge of the blade.

The mount survives the rider. When dismounted the goblin can still walk, turn, fall, and attack with the same spear. When separated from the goblin the wolf can move on its own and attack with its jaws. Use the same color pattern on the rider and on the mount in the combined version and in both separate versions. The spear flame should be brightest near the point. Leave enough dark space behind the flame for the silhouette of the weapon to stay visible during the thrust.
